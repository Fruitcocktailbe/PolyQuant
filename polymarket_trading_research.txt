# Research Report: Profitable Trading on Polymarket

## Executive Summary
Trading on Polymarket to "make it profitable" is not about gambling on outcomes; it is about structural arbitrage. The market consists of thousands of interconnected probabilities (e.g., "Trump win PA" vs. "GOP win PA" vs. "GOP win Presidency"). 

Most naive traders and simple bots look for:
1. Sum > 1 or < 1: Simple arbitrage within a single market.
2. Prices lagging news: Speed-based trading.

However, the $40 million+ opportunity described in the research papers comes from Combinatorial Arbitrage: exploiting logical inconsistencies across multiple distinct but related markets suitable for prediction markets.

## The Mathematical Foundation
Polymarket uses a CPMM (Constant Product Market Maker) and LMSR (Logarithmic Market Scoring Rule) structure. 

### Why Simple Math Fails
If you just add up prices (P(A) + P(B)), you miss the dependencies.
- Example: 
  - Market 1: "Trump wins PA" ($0.60)
  - Market 2: "Trump wins Presidency" ($0.55)
  - Logic: It is highly unlikely Trump wins PA but loses the Presidency.
  - Math: The joint probability space is constrained. A valid price vector must lie inside the Marginal Polytope (the convex hull of all valid outcomes).

### The Solution: Frank-Wolfe & Integer Programming
To find the "true" price and maximum profit, you must solve a constrained convex optimization problem.

1. Marginal Polytope: The geometric shape defining all valid probability combinations.
2. Bregman Projection: Finding the closest point in that valid shape to the current market prices (minimizing the "distance" or divergence).
3. Frank-Wolfe Algorithm: An iterative method to solve this projection without listing every possible outcome (which is 2^N and impossible to compute directly).
4. Integer Programming (IP): Used as an "oracle" inside Frank-Wolfe to find the best direction to move at each step.

## Step-by-Step Implementation Guide

### Phase 1: Infrastructure & Data
Goal: Get data faster than the block time (~2s).

1. Node Provider: Do not use public RPCs. You need a dedicated Polygon node (e.g., Alchemy, Infura, or self-hosted) to see pending transactions and new blocks immediately.
2. Market Data Ingestion:
   - CLOB API: Connect to Polymarket's Central Limit Order Book WebSocket to get real-time bids/asks.
   - Gamma API: Fetch market metadata (question, outcomes, IDs).
3. Database: Store historical ticks to backtest your parameters.

### Phase 2: Dependency Mapping (The "Brain")
Goal: Know which markets affect each other.

1. Ingest Markets: Download all active markets.
2. Identify Relations:
   - Manual Rules: e.g., "Candidate X in State Y" implies "Candidate X in National".
   - LLM Helper: Use a model (like DeepSeek or GPT-4) to parse market questions and output logical constraints (JSON format: {"A_implies_B": true, "Mutually_Exclusive": [...]}).
3. Graph Construction: Build a dependency graph where nodes are markets and edges are constraints.

### Phase 3: The Optimization Engine (The "Math")
Goal: Calculate the "Arbitrage-Free" price vector mu*.

1. Tools: Python + Gurobi (Commercial IP solver, fast) or SCIP (Open source, slower).
2. InitFW (Initialization):
   - Use the IP solver to find a set of valid starting outcomes (vertices of the polytope).
   - Compute an "interior point" u (a valid probability distribution where no probability is exactly 0 or 1).
3. Barrier Frank-Wolfe Algorithm:
   - Loop:
     1. Calculate gradient of the cost function (LMSR).
     2. Use IP solver to find the best "correction" vertex.
     3. Update your target price vector towards this correction.
     4. Adaptive Contraction: Shrink the search space slightly to prevent division-by-zero errors (LMSR fails at 0/1 probabilities).
   - Stop Condition: When Guaranteed Profit > Cost of Execution.

### Phase 4: Execution (The "Hands")
Goal: Buy/Sell the specific outcomes to lock in profit.

1. VWAP Check: Calculate the Volume-Weighted Average Price.
   - Don't just check the top bid.
   - Check: "If I buy $1000 worth, what is my average price?"
2. Profitability Threshold:
   - Calculate Projected Revenue - (Entry Cost + Gas Fees + Slippage).
   - Rule of Thumb: Only execute if ROI > 5-10% or absolute profit > $5 (due to gas/risk).
3. Atomic/Batch Transactions:
   - You must execute ALL legs of the trade. If you buy "Yes" on Market A but fail to sell "No" on Market B, you are exposed to risk.
   - Use Flashbots or transaction bundles to ensure all trades land in the same block or revert together.

## Final Warning
This is competitive PvP. The $40M profit cited was taken by sophisticated quantitative funds. You are competing against bots running on AWS closer to the exchange, using C++ or Rust for execution speed, and paying for premium Gurobi licenses.
- Start Small: Look for niche markets with fewer eyes on them.
- Focus on Logic: Simple "Sum of probabilities != 1" bots are everywhere. The real money is in complex logic (e.g., "If Player X scores > 2 goals, Team Y likely wins").
